## Model assumptions

The modelling choices your results depend on that are not derivable from tax
law or arithmetic -- places where more than one defensible answer exists and
this engine picked one -- are recorded in
[`MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md`](MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md). Start there before
interpreting any tax figure: section 5 in particular explains why turning
dividends OFF does not mean no dividends.

## Project goal

A retirement / investment projection model. Given a household's accounts, contributions, debts, spending policy, Social Security claiming choice, and tax situation, it answers whether the money lasts — under Monte Carlo simulation and under historical replay of real return sequences. It ships as **one self-contained HTML file** that runs entirely offline, installed to a phone's home screen as a PWA. No server, no third-party account aggregation, no AI integration — every number is user-typed. The user's primary device is Android; iPhone is a fully-functional supported baseline for friends, not an afterthought.

### Excluded modules — built, audited, and deliberately not shipping

**Decision register P19, 2026-09-10.** `src/mortgage-vs-investing.js` and
`src/debt-strategy-adapter.js` are **declared unsupported**. They are no longer bundled by
`build.js`, and `src/debt-payoff-strategy.js` leaves with them because nothing else in
`src/` requires it. All three remain in the repository as source; none is reachable from
the app or the Worker.

Eight of the fourteen findings from two external audit rounds live in those two modules —
six P1 defects in the comparison surface alone. They were excluded rather than repaired
because **the modules were verified unreachable**: the live engine calls only
`DebtAmortization.monthlyPayment`, and no UI ever referenced either feature.

The point of the decision is the enforcement, not the removal. Before it, these modules
were unwired *by accident* — a hand-maintained list had simply forgotten them — which is
indistinguishable from unwired on purpose. `tests/module-exclusion-registry.test.js` now
fails when any `src/debt-*.js` module is neither bound nor explicitly excluded, which
converts an accident into a decision an auditor can verify.

**Revival is governed by contract, not by preference.** The findings' failing witnesses
are deliberately retained in the suite and must go green before either module may ship
again; decisions P13, P14, P15 and P17 bind whoever revives them — an equal-funding budget
with a declared destination for residual cash, a debt-inclusive net-worth metric that never
silently flips the user's setting, horizons located by elapsed age, and a payoff status
that distinguishes *paid* from *capped* from *invalid*.

## Account types

*Verified 2026-09-10 by reading `ACCOUNT_TYPES`, the embedded 2026 rules JSON, and `withdrawFromClass()`/`ownerContributionEligibility()` directly in `src/engine.js` — not inferred from labels. For the real-world tax rules behind every type here (built or not), including the ones this table only summarizes, see [`ACCOUNT_RULES_ENGINE_REFERENCE_2026.md`](Resource%20Documents/ACCOUNT_RULES_ENGINE_REFERENCE_2026.md). **Re-verified 2026-09-16 against that document — discharged, every figure matches.** Diffed directly: the $7,500 IRA limit and $1,100 catch-up, the $153k–168k / $242k–252k Roth IRA phaseouts, the $24,500 401(k) deferral, $8,000 and $11,250 catch-ups, the $72,000 §415(c) cap, the $360,000 compensation cap, the $4,400/$8,750/$1,000 HSA figures and the $150,000 Roth-catch-up wage threshold all appear identically in the successor document. (S5 task 11 independently exercised the wage threshold and the catch-up figures the same way, landing `src/authority-status-vocabulary.json`-backed records for them.)*

Nine types, four tax classes (`taxable`, `preTax`, `roth`, `hsa`).

| Type | Tax class | 2026 contribution limit | Withdrawal treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Taxable brokerage | `taxable` | none | *(Corrected 2026-09-24, S5AA workstream B `235eb5f`: basis is tracked in **dollars** per account; `basisPct` is only the opening input. Growth adds no basis; contributions and reinvested dividends do. The sentence "One blended percentage per account" below no longer describes it; "not per-lot" still holds.)* Real cost-basis tracking (`basisPct` per account) — each withdrawal splits into gains vs. basis, gains taxed as capital gains. One blended percentage per account, not per-lot — a real simplification specifically for DRIP-heavy accounts, where each reinvestment is its own tax lot with its own basis and holding-period clock (a decade of quarterly reinvestment ≈ 40 lots, corrected 2026-09-16 from 41); see `MARKET_DATA_ENGINE_REFERENCE_2026.md` |
| Custom taxable account | `taxable` | none | Same as above, an unlabeled escape hatch |
| Traditional IRA | `preTax` | $7,500, shared with Roth IRA, +$1,100 catch-up at 50+ | Ordinary income; RMD applies (real IRS Uniform Lifetime Table, start age by birth year — 73/75, with the 1959-cohort regulatory ambiguity explicitly flagged) — a pre-tax distribution counts toward it whatever it lands in, including a transfer into a taxable account or an HSA (S5AA R30, `MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md` §21.1); 10% penalty if age <59.5, unless rule-of-55 or the penalty-exception toggle *(Corrected 2026-09-24, checked against the engine at `fab88f0`: the 10% applies only to the taxable part of an IRA withdrawal, on the account owner's age; the rule of 55 never exempts an IRA, only a workplace plan; the penalty-exception toggle still applies)* |

| Traditional 401(k) | `preTax` | $24,500 deferral shared with Roth 401(k); +$8,000 catch-up (50+) or $11,250 "super" catch-up (ages 60–63, SECURE 2.0); $72,000 total employee+employer cap; $360,000 compensation cap | Same as traditional IRA *(except that the rule of 55 does apply to it, on the account owner's age; corrected 2026-09-24)* |
| Roth 401(k) | `roth` | shared with traditional 401(k) above | Tax-free; correctly **not** income-phased-out (unlike Roth IRA) |
| Custom tax-deferred account | `preTax` | none (manual, no limit enforcement) | Same as traditional |
| Custom tax-free account | `roth` | none (manual, no limit enforcement) | Same as Roth |
| Health Savings Account | `hsa` | $4,400 self / $8,750 family, +$1,000 catch-up at 55+ (correctly not inflation-indexed); family base is a genuinely shared pool across spouses (SA-05 fixed an eligibility-before-allocation bug here) | **Unconditionally tax-free and penalty-free — see gap below** |

Limit enforcement is real, not cosmetic: IRA and 401(k) limits are keyed per owner (`group+":"+owner`) and correctly shared between each traditional/Roth pair. The engine also tracks SECURE 2.0's mandatory-Roth-catch-up-for-high-earners wage threshold ($150k) as an explicit flag, currently marked not-yet-mandatory for 2026.

**Confirmed tax-rule gaps (found 2026-09-10 tracing `withdrawFromClass()`, which branches only on `taxClass==="taxable"` and `taxClass==="preTax"`):**
- **HSA withdrawals have no qualified-vs-non-qualified distinction** — every HSA withdrawal is treated as tax-free and penalty-free regardless of purpose or age. Real law: ordinary income tax **plus a 20% penalty** (not the 10% already coded for `preTax`) on non-qualified withdrawals before 65; ordinary income only, no penalty, after 65. *(**Repaired 2026-09-20, S5AA task 4.4 (Q99):** a qualified-medical share (per account, optional, default 100%) now splits every HSA withdrawal, taxed as the law describes. Since S5AA R29 (2026-09-28), this also covers a transfer out of an HSA into any other account — `MODEL_ASSUMPTIONS.md` §21. This line is kept as history; it no longer describes the engine.)*
- **Roth withdrawals (IRA and 401(k)) have no 5-year-rule or contribution/conversion/earnings ordering logic** — also unconditionally tax-free. A smaller miss than the HSA one for a long-horizon retirement model, but a real simplification worth naming.
- These are rule-fidelity gaps on account types that already exist, distinct from the missing account types below.

**Not yet built** (full rules for every item below are in [`ACCOUNT_RULES_ENGINE_REFERENCE_2026.md`](Resource%20Documents/ACCOUNT_RULES_ENGINE_REFERENCE_2026.md)):
- 403(b) — reuses the `workplace` limit rules exactly (identical 2026 figures to 401(k) in real law), plus one 403(b)-only wrinkle: a 15-years-of-service catch-up at certain qualifying employers, independent of the age-based catch-ups
- 457(b) governmental — confirmed genuinely separate, not a relabeled 401(k): $24,500 deferral in 2026 that does **not** share room with a 401(k)/403(b) (someone with both could defer $49,000 combined before catch-ups), plus a **structural exemption from the 10% early-withdrawal penalty at any age** — not an "exception," a different rule entirely
- 457(b) non-governmental ("top-hat") — same separate $24,500 limit, but **no** age-50+ catch-up permitted (only a special final-3-years catch-up), and materially different distribution mechanics than governmental 457(b) despite the shared name — under-researched, flag for follow-up if ever prioritized
- SEP-IRA — employer-only, lesser of 25% of compensation or $72,000 (2026), effective self-employed rate closer to ~20% of net self-employment income due to the deduction's circular math
- SIMPLE IRA — $17,000 deferral (2026), with a confirmed real anomaly: the "enhanced" small-employer plan tier's 50-59/64+ catch-up ($3,850) is *lower* than the regular plan's ($4,000), an apparent unintended COLA-rounding interaction, not a typo
- Inherited/beneficiary IRA — the SECURE Act 10-year drawdown rule, plus annual RMDs *within* that window now actually enforced (as of distribution year 2025) when the original owner died on/after their own RMD age — this took three rounds of IRS enforcement delays (2022-2024) before landing here
- 529 — already tracked below (Education/family); a fundamentally different tax shape (federal gift-tax-exclusion-bounded, not a dollar limit; state aggregate caps; the SECURE 2.0 $35k lifetime 529-to-Roth-IRA rollover)
- Cash-value life insurance — currently only a flat-growth `lifeCashValue` "other asset," not an account with loan/withdrawal/MEC tax treatment

**Withdrawal optimizer** — replacing what `withdrawalOrder:"optimized"` and its `optimizationGoal` presets (`balanced`/`taxes`/`success`/`spending` in the schema; user-facing as balanced, minimum lifetime taxes, lifetime withdrawal safety, and maximum lifetime spending) actually do. Today's `smartWithdrawalOrder()` (`src/engine.js`) is a fixed ranking, not an optimizer: each tax class starts from a hand-picked score, a handful of rules nudge those scores per year (age vs. 59.5/RMD-start, IRMAA proximity, goal, debt load, survivor timing…), and the classes drain in sorted order for the *entire* year's shortfall — it cannot split one year's withdrawal across classes (e.g. fill the 12% bracket from preTax, take the rest from taxable) and has no lookahead, so "minimize taxes" can raise lifetime tax by building up RMDs, Social Security taxability, and IRMAA exposure it never sees coming. **Measured, not just theorized:** an ad-hoc QA sweep across 5 presets × 3 stress-designed household shapes found the presets collapse to identical or near-identical output far more often than they differentiate — 2 of 3 shapes showed near-total collapse across all five, including one where "minimize taxes" and "protect legacy" produced byte-identical results — because each preset's per-class nudge (single digits to teens) is dwarfed by goal-independent signals already in the same scoring function (a +45 pre-59.5 penalty, -14 to -20 for RMD proximity, +18 for the IRMAA guard); see `SIMULATION_LOG.md` Batch 17 and Batch 24, and `SPRINT_QUESTIONS.md` Q56 (committed as of `58c7a78`) — filed there as rebuild design input, not as a defect to repair in the current system. Replacement: a per-year policy (bracket-fill target, 0%-capital-gains harvest, an IRMAA ceiling, a Roth/HSA reserve floor, Social Security claim age per spouse, pension election, and — scoped to annuities the household already owns, not a buy decision — annuity income) searched per preset across Monte Carlo/historical paths using common random numbers (the engine's RNG is already seeded — `rng-seeding.test.js` — so paired policy comparisons need far fewer paths than independent runs), verified on small deterministic scenarios against an LP tax-minimization lower bound before it's trusted. **On the WebGPU compute path, recorded rather than silently dropped.** It was decided against on 2026-09-10, after measurement showed the simulation core spends ~95% of its time on object churn rather than arithmetic, and that the aggregation hot loop can be made 26–42× faster by replacing a full sort with a selection — bit-identically, on the CPU, with no GPU involved. What remained for a GPU was a full WGSL rewrite of the simulation core, which would mean maintaining two engines at different precision (WGSL has no float64), two shader variants for Adreno and Mali, and a mandatory CPU fallback anyway since Android WebGPU coverage is ~70–78%. The engine rebuild is expected to reach ~0.8 s at 10,000 paths on an iPhone 15 Pro without it. **It has a revisit trigger, not a closure:** if *generalized decision heatmaps* on this same list get built, they need a Monte Carlo run per grid cell — a 20×20 grid at 1,000 paths is ~400,000 path-simulations, roughly 32 s on CPU against ~3 s on GPU. Heatmaps are the feature that would reopen this. The rebuilt engine's architecture (phase-split, columnar, path-fastest ordering) keeps that door open at no extra cost.
