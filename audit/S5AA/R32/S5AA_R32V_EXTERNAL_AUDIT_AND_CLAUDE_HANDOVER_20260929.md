## 4. Primary-source checks behind the verdicts

**A — Social Security survivors, spouses and COLAs.** [20 CFR 404.338](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.338) and [POMS RS 00615.320](https://secure.ssa.gov/poms.nsf/lnx/0300615320) support the survivor age reduction and widow(er) limit ordering. [20 CFR 404.335](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.335) requires the worker's insured status, not a prior retirement claim. [20 CFR 404.333](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.333) defines the spouse's benefit, including reduction/dual entitlement; it does not grant spousal delayed retirement credits. [20 CFR 404.212(d)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.212) and [404.271](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.271) support post-eligibility COLAs. Precise eligibility, birth months and entitled months remain necessary for precise legal payments.

**B — Earnings test.** [20 CFR 404.429](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.429), [42 USC 411(a)(11)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/42/411) and [20 CFR 404.435](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.435) support net SE earnings and the first grace year's monthly rule. The [SSA earnings-test amounts](https://www.ssa.gov/oact/cola/rtea.html) verify $24,480/$65,160 for 2026. Monthly retirement/nonservice conditions must actually be satisfied; annual earnings alone do not decide that exception.

**C — RMD eligibility.** [26 CFR 1.401(a)(9)-5](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.401%28a%29%289%29-5) and [Publication 590-B, Table II](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590b) require the more-than-ten-years-younger spouse to be the sole beneficiary; the 75/64 denominator is 25.3. [IRC 401(a)(9)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/401) supplies the still-working exception and exclusions. Future spousal succession is not enough to establish today's beneficiary designation.

**D — Medicare.** [20 CFR 418.1115](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/418.1115) and [418.1135](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/418.1135) pair the applicable income ranges with tax-return status and MAGI. [CMS's 2026 Part B/Part D tables](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2026-medicare-parts-b-premiums-deductibles) confirm the amounts and inclusive final threshold. A life-changing-event adjustment can change the tax year used; it does not justify pairing old joint MAGI with a new single bracket.

**E — IRA contributions.** [IRC 219](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/219), especially (b), (c), (g), and [IRC 408A(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/408A) support own/spousal compensation limits and phaseout ordering. [Publication 590-A, Worksheets 1-2 and 2-2](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p590a) provide a useful independent calculation of the reduced limits, lesser-of order, minimum and rounding. Removing an IRA work-age proxy does not remove compensation, filing, coverage or MAGI requirements.

**F — Workplace contributions/vesting.** [IRC 415(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/415), [414(v)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/414) and [402(g)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/402) separate compensation, catch-up and excess-deferral rules. [IRC 411(a)(2)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/411) provides minimum vesting schedules tied to years of service; it does not prescribe the owner's proposed interpolation from any entered percentage.

**G — Aged deductions and carryover.** [IRC 63(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/63) and [151(d)(5)(C)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/151) use age attained by year-end; a spouse's senior deduction requires joint filing, and a married individual must file jointly for that senior deduction. [IRC 1212(b)(2)(B)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1212) expressly adds back section 151 in adjusted taxable income and permits negative taxable income for this calculation. Thus the 2025-enacted senior deduction matters to carryover even though its location on the return differs from an old personal exemption.

**H — Distribution exceptions.** [IRC 72(t)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/72) and the [IRS exception table](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-topics-exceptions-to-tax-on-early-distributions) require the qualifying separation-year/account conditions for Rule of 55. [IRC 223(f)(4)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/223) gives the HSA age-65 additional-tax exception. Timing facts for an actual distribution cannot be manufactured by splitting an undated annual draw after the fact.

## 5. The owner's decisions: legal and implementation qualifications

The decisions remain the owner's. These are refinements to make the chosen behavior accurate and reviewable, not new approvals or substituted decisions.

| Decision | Review |
|---|---|
| 1 — Never-claimed worker survivor | The deceased need not have filed. Use the correct survivor base and DRCs actually earned before death, then the applicable survivor reduction/limits. Keeping Q3b's own-claim-age recipient gate is a **model restriction**: statutory survivor eligibility can start at 60 independently of the survivor's own retirement claim. Label that surviving limitation; do not describe the entire resulting system as unrestricted statutory survivor benefits. |
| 2 — Spousal benefit | Sound direction. Implement own benefit plus eligible spousal excess with its own reduction; total is not simply own benefit plus half the worker's PIA. Retain filing/eligibility conditions, no spousal DRC, and appropriate bounds for family maximum/deemed filing or clearly disclosed unsupported cases. |
| 3 — Today's-dollar Social Security input | A legitimate unit convention, not dictated by SSA law. Relabel the input; prevent double growth of estimates already given in claim-year dollars. For AIME, eligibility-to-entitlement COLAs follow statutory timing, with PIA rounding; applying a chosen annual future COLA is still a projection assumption. |
| 4 — Own full, joint half basis | A reasonable explicit ownership approximation. [IRC 1014(a), (b)(6)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/1014) gives fair-market-value basis, which can step **down** as well as up. Sole ownership must denote eligible property acquired from the decedent; retirement/other income-in-respect-of-decedent exceptions remain. Joint-half is an assumed decedent share, not a universal titling rule. Qualifying community property can reset both halves. Set inherited basis to eligible date-of-death value, including losses, rather than merely raising basis. |
| 5a — Each owner's contribution-stop age | Coherent with owner work clocks and cures SA32F-12. Apply consistently to owner-specific accounts and define joint-account timing. Also specify whose age labels future contribution changes use; the stop-age decision alone does not settle every schedule field. |
| 5b — Vest over six years | An allowed model convention if accurately labelled. Six years of **credited service** is not necessarily six years from projection start, nor a statute requiring linear interpolation. Decide how existing service/vested percentage maps to the chosen schedule. Track vested/unvested employer money and forfeit only the relevant unvested balance at modeled separation. Avoid growing already discarded deposits or assuming every retirement is a forfeiture event. |
| 5c — Allow spousal IRA while joint pay | Sound if a joint return and sufficient combined qualifying compensation support both spouses' contributions. Retain individual annual caps, phaseouts and nondeductible basis accounting. Workplace contributions remain subject to their contributing employee's own compensation rules. |
| 6 — Inflate fixed-nominal spending to retirement | Coherent: grow today's-dollar base through the retirement date, then hold that nominal amount. Specify partial retirement timing and stage units. This is a chosen spending convention. |
| 7 — Average-return Monte Carlo disclosure | Keeping arithmetic mean is coherent. Say simple mode is a deterministic constant-return illustration and explain why the Monte Carlo median differs. Avoid promising it is the actual mean of all projected outcomes: clamps, account shocks, withdrawals, nonlinear taxes and allocation changes matter. A deposit-only unclamped one-account iid control can illustrate the basic distinction. |
| 8 — Index later law in its own round | Sound as an explicitly labelled forecast convention. Do not claim plan inflation reproduces all statutory indexes: ordinary thresholds use their legal inflation rules; SSA wage/bend-point measures use wage indexing; other provisions are fixed. Keep SS taxable-benefit bases, NIIT thresholds and the $3,000 loss limit fixed where law fixes them. Senior-deduction sunset after 2028 and other scheduled-law changes are separate from indexation; the current owner's carried extension policy must be reconciled explicitly. |

## 6. Additional R32V findings

### R32V-01 — P2: simple-mode spending decisions use an unused headline return

1. **Evidence:** at frozen 3017351f303b5939f610a00c283ad243350f4418, a simple plan with assetsOn, one 100%-allocated Roth account and asset return −10% has actual negative market growth, but flexibility does not cut the following year's spending because headline returnRate is +10%.
2. **Code:** E:3201 accountReturnForPeriod uses allocated returns; E:3982 assigns simple-mode priorReturn from assumptions.returnRate; E:3065 cuts spending when priorReturn is negative. The same signal also enters Guyton/optimized ordering. Q109 describes an immediately preceding-period return trigger; no located disclosure says an unused flat rate controls this asset-class case.
3. **Reproduction:** adjacent script, age 70–73, $1M Roth, $40,000 nominal spending, 10% flexibility, zero fees/inflation/volatility. After the first −10% asset year, **$40,000 × 90% = $36,000 expected; $40,000 actual**, +$4,000. Changing only the unused headline rate to −10% leaves the first-period portfolio identical but makes second-period spending $36,000. Conversely, +10% asset return/−10% headline return wrongly cuts to $36,000 instead of $40,000.
4. **Consequence/reach:** annual spending guidance and withdrawals move in either direction without a changed investment return. One-account supported cases reached; corpus membership not measured. This is distinct from SA32F-37's prospective VPW rate.
5. **Proposed repair:** define a consistent preceding-period portfolio return signal for asset-class plans and use it across methods. With one invested account, its modeled return's sign is unambiguous. The owner should choose the multi-account weighting and treatment of cash/fees; test gain, loss and zero-return controls before repair. Do not use the portfolio's balance decline from spending as a market-return proxy.

### R32V-02 — P3: the tax sweep reference repeats the capital-loss legal omission

1. **Evidence:** the claimed independent reference agrees with the engine at carry $10,000 where §1212 requires $8,150. The sweep's 8,070 matches therefore cannot support a clean carryover claim.
2. **Code:** SA32F/TAX-FED/ref.js, lossUsed formula (line 48 in the published file); compare E:2259. The reference subtracts the full senior deduction without the required §151 add-back.
3. **Reproduction:** adjacent script calls both implementations with single age 70, $20,000 ordinary pension, no gains/dividends/SS and $10,000 loss. AGI $17,000 − deductions $24,150 = −$7,150 TI; adjusted TI = −$7,150 + $3,000 + $6,000 = $1,850. Carry = $10,000 − $1,850 = **$8,150**. **Both return $10,000**, $1,850 too high.
4. **Consequence/reach:** false assurance in a legal reference/check. The underlying model dollar error remains SA32F-34 and is not counted again. Boundary ages 64/65 and nonjoint spouse combinations also lie outside the sweep's effective grid.
5. **Proposed repair:** independently fix the audit reference from §1212, add this diagonal case and statutory worksheet comments, then rerun the sweep. A clean count must identify shared assumptions and excluded cases.

### R32V-03 — P2: Social Security rounding is omitted from precise dollar outputs

1. **Evidence:** the SOCSEC area report already mentions missing PIA dime rounding as an unnumbered minor observation. R32V confirms it and the subsequent whole-dollar payment gap, omitted from the consolidated 55.
2. **Code:** E:1990 ssaBenefitAtClaim carries the raw bend-point sum straight to annual benefit. Later COLA/period income paths also preserve fractional payments rather than implementing the statutory sequence.
3. **Reproduction:** with AIME $5,825 under the frozen 2026 bend points, .9 × 1,286 + .32 × (5,825 − 1,286) = **$2,609.88**. PIA truncates to **$2,609.80**. At FRA with no COLA, that gives $31,317.60 annual PIA versus **$31,318.56** in the helper. With no Medicare deduction, final monthly payment truncates to **$2,609**, or **$31,308/year**; runPlan income is **$31,318.56**, +$10.56. This isolates rounding within the frozen-year approximation, not the cohort/indexing convention.
4. **Consequence/reach:** narrow dollar overstatement, with later COLAs/reductions affected by sequencing; P2 under the narrower-figure rubric, despite the area note's P3 label. No corpus reach measured.
5. **Proposed repair:** implement/document the PIA/COLA/benefit/payment rounding sequence at the correct stages. [20 CFR 404.212(c)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/20/404.212), [SSA's worked 2026 example](https://www.ssa.gov/OACT/ProgData/retirebenefit2.html), and [POMS RS 00601.020](https://secure.ssa.gov/POMS.NSF/LNX/0300601020) distinguish these stages. Medicare deductions and reduction rounding must not be collapsed into a blanket early annual-dollar truncation.

The seven gross-up cases establish selected solver agreement with the stated reference; they do not validate every tax component or missing QDCG cap. Worker parity and exact result keys establish consistency, not independent financial correctness. Resolve the qualified eligibility/precedence/calendar points before selecting a repair expectation. Retain the owner's choices with the qualifications in §5; do not replace those choices or silently broaden the scope. Each repaired financial case needs a hand-derived expectation and an opposite/control case; then measure affected corpus members and rerun the supported Windows gate. This report makes no claim that the unresolved model is GO merely because its existing tests pass.
