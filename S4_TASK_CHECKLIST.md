## §R. The re-scope, and the reason for it

**S4 was "2026 rules conformance." It is now "build the instrument."** The entire tax body moved to S5; S5's oracle-integrity work and two of S6's read-only registers moved here.

### The reason is a dependency, not a preference

The previous S4's own precondition read:

> *"Capture a pre-sprint baseline via `tools/capture-baseline.js` — tasks 2, 3, 4, 6 and 8 all move financial output by design."*

That instrument has had **five separate defects repaired across two audit rounds** — EXT-02, ST2-02, CL-03, CR2-04, CR2-05 — and an external auditor found every one of them. Four of the five were not arithmetic errors. They were *the instrument reaching a verdict without looking at the thing it claims to describe*: an index that collided, a corpus that was partial and certified anyway, a completeness check placed after the shortcut that made it unreachable.

**The old S5 task 0 is what makes that instrument trustworthy — and it was scheduled after S4.** So the plan had a sprint that moves financial output in four separate tasks depending on a verification scheduled to happen afterwards. That is the inversion this re-scope fixes.

### The second reason: the differential harness had no proving ground

The differential harness was the **last task of the last preparatory sprint**. Its only proof was to be old-vs-old plus a deliberately-injected fault. Moved here, it is built on a quiet tree and then **every output-moving commit in S5 exercises it for real**, each with a diff predicted in writing beforehand. A real change with a predicted shape is a far better proof than a synthetic one, and it costs nothing — S5 has to predict those movements anyway.

### The third reason: two registers whose output is a list of work

The requirements register produces an **`UNGUARDED` count** — behaviours a rebuild could silently lose. The test classification produces a list of **behaviours guarded only by implementation-coupled tests**, each of which needs an implementation-independent equivalent *written before the rebuild starts*. Both are read-only harvests. Both were scheduled last. Discovering a list of work at the end of the last preparatory sprint leaves no room to do the work.

### The fourth reason: the longest-lead item was not started first

The device benchmark harness touches no engine code, and its critical measurement needs a **real iPhone** — the one deliverable in this entire plan whose date is set by someone other than us. It has now moved S6 → S5 → S4. It is a parallel track from day one.

## §N. The counts in this sprint, and how to refresh them

**The owner's decision, 2026-09-12: the numbers stay.** *"i like the numbers even if they need to be updated frequently."* So this checklist carries them, and this section is how they get refreshed without anyone deriving one who should not.

### Every count here is stamped with the commit it was true at

`thirteen (as of `c938952`)` rather than `thirteen`. **A commit stamp provides traceability, not proof** — it tells a reader which state a figure described and lets them re-derive it against that state. **It does not establish the figure was correct for that state.** What it buys is that the number is self-reporting: a reader who sees a stamp six rounds old knows to re-derive, where a bare number gives no signal at all. Two stamped numbers in two documents need a glance to compare; two bare ones need a reconciliation.

*Corrected 2026-09-12 at the external auditor's request. This section previously said "a stamped number is never wrong, only dated," which claims more than the convention earns and contradicts the limit stated two paragraphs below. The convention survives intact; only the claim about it shrinks.*

This is the fix for the failure this project keeps paying for. Q37's sprint numbering, the CL-01 tracker entries, the audit banner that sat critical through five closed rounds, an adjacent session telling the user *"package `61382c7` — this is the one to send"* eight rounds after it stopped being true, and this subtask's own earlier text. **Every one was a true-when-written claim with no date attached.** The repair each time was to attach the date, not to remove the claim.

**A stamp is necessary and not sufficient, and that limit is the reason the sentence above was corrected.** It tells a reader *which state* the claim described; it does not tell them the claim was *right about that state*. **Three questions, three answers: the stamp says WHEN, the register name says OF WHAT, and only a derivation says WHETHER IT WAS CORRECT.** Three sessions spent 2026-09-11 making confident claims from single fixtures, and every one was caught by another session re-running it — not by anyone noticing a stale date.

### Do not derive these counts here

The counting rule lives in **`ROADMAP_EXTERNAL_REVIEW.md`** — *"How findings are counted"* (added at `3f32509`) and *"Counting: the rule needed sharpening, and then needed a boundary"* (`40d7afe`). It says: **count once per distinct mechanism at first discovery-and-repair**, with a boundary that **a regression always counts** even inside the mechanism of the repair that created it.

**Applying it requires judgements this checklist does not own** — whether CR2-05 reopening ST2-02's completeness gate is one mechanism refined or two, for instance. On 2026-09-12 three sessions were asked for these figures and **all three declined**, each saying they would get exactly that fold wrong. The session that owns the rule supplied them with its working.

**And do not reach for a grep.** Both obvious ones are wrong in opposite directions, measured: an ID grep of the *ledger* **under-counts** (several comparator findings never contain the literal string `capture-baseline`), while an ID grep of the *source file* **over-counts** (26 IDs named, 15 actually located there; `build.js` names 14, 7 located). An earlier version of this section was refreshed from a grep whose pattern was a hand-written list of the IDs already believed relevant — **which could only return what was put into it**, and missed P9-01, RA-04, P6-02 and P7-03.

- [ ] **To refresh:** ask the session that owns the audit ledger, cite the commit they derived at, and stamp it. Do not compute it here, and do not accept a figure without a commit

### Name the register as well as the commit

**The stamp says *when*. The register name says *of what*.** Both are needed, and the second is the one that stops a total absorbing someone else's work.

*"We closed 59"* can absorb another session's output. *"59 in the S2 closure register (as of `c938952`)"* cannot — because it never claimed to be anyone's output in the first place. **A count is a property of the register, not of a workstream**: it answers *how many distinct mechanisms the audits have established in this model*, and that has one answer regardless of who did the work. The moment a total becomes per-session there are N totals and the reconciliation problem returns — documents disagreeing not because anyone is wrong but because they are counting different things.

So: every figure below names its register and its commit, and neither is optional.

## Ground rules

1. Every task states its own gate. Not done until the gate passes **and** `npm test` is green.
2. Per-task durations are **floors, not targets.** A task genuinely bigger than its floor is information — record it, keep going.
3. **No task may weaken an existing test to pass.** If an existing test and a document vector disagree, **stop and record it as a question.**
4. Any figure from a specification is cited in code by document and section.
5. Authority status is a **typed field**, never prose in a comment. *(The vocabulary is chosen in S5 task 4; this sprint creates no tax parameters, so nothing here needs it yet.)*
6. Re-locate by symbol, not by line. Record drift.
7. Commit per task. *(Amended 2026-09-13 per S4-PA-09: commit per **reviewable unit** — instrument changes, corpus changes and engine changes never share a commit, and a large task such as 2b is several commits. Task 1.3's renormalise commit stays isolated.)*
8. **Record findings rather than fix them** where a fix requires a policy judgment.
9. **Nothing in this sprint may move financial output.** That is not a coincidence — it is the point. Every task here builds or verifies an instrument, and an instrument that changes its subject while measuring it is useless. If a task in this sprint moves a fixture, that is a **finding**, not a result to accept. *(**Amended 2026-09-13 per S4-PA-03 — the rule, made precise:** for identical validated inputs, seed, rules/data, engine source and execution contract, supported financial results must not change. **New corpus members and evidence metadata may be added or versioned without replacing historical evidence.** A **rejection-policy** change is a behaviour change even if no valid-input amount moves.
